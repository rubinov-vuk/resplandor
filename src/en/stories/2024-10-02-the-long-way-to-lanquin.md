The woman selling tamales said eight, and crossed herself. The driver, when I finally found him, said only *ahorita*, which in Guatemala can mean five minutes or the rest of your life.

## I. The first bus

The first bus was a retired American school bus, painted in colours that do not exist in nature. It left when it was full, and it was full when there was no air left between the passengers. A boy sold oranges through the window at every bend; a woman beside me slept with her head on a sack of beans, and later, without asking, on my shoulder.

By Cobán the road had stopped pretending to be a road. We changed to a pickup truck, then to a minibus with a goat tied to the roof rack, then to another pickup, standing in the back with eleven strangers and the evening coming down green over the hills.

[photo 2]

## II. The river

Lanquín arrived all at once: a handful of lights, a river loud in the dark, and the smell of wet earth. I had been travelling for eleven hours and had spent less than the price of a coffee in Belgrade. Somewhere in that sum is the whole reason I keep going.
